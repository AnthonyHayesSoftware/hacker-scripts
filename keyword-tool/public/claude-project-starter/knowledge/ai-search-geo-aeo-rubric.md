# AI search scoring rubric — GEO / AEO (/100)

GEO = Generative Engine Optimisation. AEO = Answer Engine Optimisation. This
measures how likely the page is to be **surfaced and cited** by AI answer
engines (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Copilot).

## 1. Answer-first structure — 20 pts
- Each section opens with a direct, self-contained answer (1–3 sentences) that
  reads correctly if quoted in isolation. (10)
- A concise summary / "key takeaways" near the top. (5)
- Question-shaped headings that mirror how people ask. (5)

## 2. Extractability — 20 pts
- Facts in clean, liftable units: short sentences, definitions, steps. (8)
- Tables and lists for comparisons, specs, and processes. (7)
- One idea per paragraph; no burying the answer mid-paragraph. (5)

## 3. Evidence & trust signals — 20 pts
- Specific, verifiable claims with numbers, dates, named sources. (8)
- Citations / links to authoritative references. (6)
- Clear authorship, expertise, and recency signals. (6)

## 4. Topical completeness — 15 pts
- Answers the obvious follow-up questions on the page. (8)
- Covers entities and subtopics an AI would expect for the topic. (7)

## 5. Structured data & semantics — 15 pts
- Appropriate schema (FAQPage, HowTo, Article, Product). (7)
- Semantic HTML: real headings, lists, tables (not div soup). (4)
- Descriptive anchors and alt text that add context. (4)

## 6. Uniqueness & quotability — 10 pts
- Original data, examples, or framing an AI can't get elsewhere. (6)
- Memorable, citable statements and definitions. (4)

---
**How AI engines pick sources:** they favour content that *directly answers*,
is *easy to extract*, and is *trustworthy*. Optimise for being the cleanest,
most quotable answer — not the longest page.

**Banding:** 90–100 strong citation candidate · 75–89 likely with edits ·
60–74 partial · <60 unlikely to be cited.
